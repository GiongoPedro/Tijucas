About the Festival

Tijucas actively participated in the creation of Olhar de Cinema during the festival's first three years, 2012, 2013, and 2014. We were responsible for creating the communication and visual identity (the now-classic 'little glasses'), one of its most striking features. The early editions carried a highly provocative proposal that pulled the audience out of the common ground in a conceptual and refined manner. Combined with quality screenings and bold curation, this quickly put the event on the map of the most relevant film festivals in Brazil.

Tijucas was also an official supporter of the festival in its crucial first edition, lending the necessary energy for the emergence of this great event in the city of Curitiba.

Olhar de Cinema 2012

In 2012, Olhar de Cinema began its activities as an international independent film festival. In that first edition, we primarily aimed to present the festival to the public as an event focused on auteur and bold films. Curitiba had previously hosted several film festivals, but they were eventually discontinued, leaving a void for both us as filmmakers and the audience. Creating a festival was a huge desire for everyone, so we poured a lot of our energy into it.

The guiding concept of the 1st edition of Olhar de Cinema was to connect this festival-hungry audience to the bold films we proposed to screen. We conceived as an initial reference a crowd staring back at us in the dark cinema hall, as if we were the screen itself. At that moment, we recalled Tarsila do Amaral's painting, Os Operários (1933). We saw the audiovisual sector as an industry, and just as any industry has its workers, here we had the workers of the eyes and of the making—who need to watch films and make them, with their expert eyes.

The visual identity, the logo, was also inspired by the idea of 'looking'. The glasses were a way to represent something beyond the eye itself—it is the lens of the gaze, like the lens of the camera. It is a technical apparatus that makes us see beyond. Some say the identity was inspired by childhood games, when we spent hours looking for lost glasses in "Where's Wally?" books. Training the eye, looking for details. These glasses were found in our subconscious and placed in the festival's identity.

Olhar de Cinema 2013

Faced with the massive response of the audience, which packed all sessions in the 2012 edition, in 2013 we took a leap forward and the festival grew. This was the year that confirmed Olhar de Cinema – Curitiba International Film Festival as a restless, bold event whose master line encompasses the ideas of LEAP / RISK, making it clear that there is no room for complacency here.

For the second edition, the concept we developed together with the festival's artistic directors was that it takes immense courage to make cinema. And indeed it does; sometimes a film is made by rowing against the tide. We thought of images of people taking risks. Even animals took their leaps—the fish making a death leap out of the aquarium, the cat's leap. Yves Klein strongly inspired us with his leap into the void and his shade of blue. The diver plunging into the deep to find treasure ended up being chosen for the main poster. This was a very beautiful and powerful edition. The highly successful marketing communicated the festival and brought a great air of novelty to the events held in the city. At night, the billboard of the diving suit lit up, creating a highly cinematic illusion.

Olhar de Cinema 2014

  • The idea of the plant arose alongside an inquiry on which types of fruits would result from the planting, growing and rooting of Olhar de Cinema.
  • After numerous researches and a deep plunge into the festival's flora we stumbled upon a curious plant, the so called Olhares Papoulus Sapindales, also known by the Tupi indians as the Eçá-eçá* of the bush.
  • With an incredible power to grow, proliferate and to adapt, the Olhares Papoulus Sapindales may grow and thrive in any region of Brazil or the world, yet remains scarce thanks to those damn weeds.
  • * Eçá = Eye. The eyes. To see. To spy.
